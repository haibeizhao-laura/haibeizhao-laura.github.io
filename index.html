Welcome! I’m Haibei (Laura) Zhao, a master’s student in the Department of Statistics and Data Science at the University of California, Los Angeles, where I work under the supervision of Yuhua Zhu. I’m also a research assistant at Olin Business School, Washington University in St. Louis, where I work with Arlen Dean on healthcare operations management. Before that, I earned my bachelor’s degree in Management Science at Xiamen University. Before returning to academia, I worked on biotech and healthcare investments in venture capital, followed by data pipelines and analytics in private equity.

My research interests center on healthcare information systems and decision support: how data-driven tools can help healthcare organizations make decisions under uncertainty. My prior work in biotech investing shapes the practical questions I ask, while my training in statistics, reinforcement learning, and optimization shapes how I approach them.

I’m always happy to connect and exchange ideas about research, data, or shared interests.

Research Interests

  • Healthcare information systems & decision support: I am interested in how healthcare organizations turn predictions into decisions: when algorithmic recommendations help clinicians and operators, how they change workflows, and how decision policies should adapt as new information arrives.

Healthcare operations management

[slides]
  • Healthcare

Olin Business School, Washington University in St. Louis

Research Assistant · Advised by Arlen Dean

I study how to schedule follow-up visits for patients receiving treatment for geographic atrophy (GA), an advanced form of dry age-related macular degeneration. At each visit, the return interval affects the timing of the next examination and, if appropriate, the next dose. This makes scheduling a sequence of decisions rather than a fixed calendar choice.

Our work examines how the location of remaining retinal tissue, treatment history, treatment-associated neovascular risk, and visit burden may change the value of returning sooner or later. I am developing a decision model to make these trade-offs explicit and to study how the preferred interval may change as information arrives.
