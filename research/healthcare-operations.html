Treatment-interval decisions for geographic atrophy

How should the timing of the next visit change as a patient’s disease state and treatment history evolve?

Ongoing work · Public overview

01 · Clinical setting

Why geographic atrophy?

Geographic atrophy (GA) is an advanced form of dry age-related macular degeneration. As damaged areas of the retina expand, central vision can be affected.

DISEASE

Progressive tissue loss

The location of remaining tissue matters: damage near the center of vision can have different functional consequences from damage elsewhere.

TREATMENT

Benefits and risks

Complement-inhibitor injections can slow GA lesion growth. Treatment is also associated with a risk of neovascular AMD, which requires monitoring.

Clinical context: National Eye Institute · Syfovre U.S. prescribing information

02 · Decision setting

The next visit is a decision

At a visit, the care team observes the current state and chooses when to see the patient again. The return date affects examination timing and, if appropriate, the next treatment.

03 · Research question

What should guide the interval?

As the retinal status and treatment history change, when is an earlier return worth its costs, and when is a later return preferable?
  • How much protectable tissue remains, and where is it?
  • How might the next interval change treatment benefit, neovascular risk, and detection timing?
  • How should these considerations be balanced with the burden of additional visits?

These are motivating research questions. The modeling assumptions, methods, and results are under development.
