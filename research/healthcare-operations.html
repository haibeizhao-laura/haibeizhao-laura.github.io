01 · Clinical background

Dry and wet

AMD affects the macula. As the disease advances, an eye can develop geographic atrophy, neovascular AMD, or both.

Early AMD→Intermediate AMD→Late AMD

· Late dry AMD

Retinal tissue is lost in patches. Lesions can enlarge toward the fovea and impair central vision.

Our research starts here: before the first CNV event in the treated eye.

· Wet AMD

Abnormal blood vessels can leak fluid or blood and damage the macula. Anti-VEGF treatment helps control leakage.

Clinical management changes if CNV is detected.

If develops in an eye with GA, the atrophy remains. A scheduling decision can affect when the event is detected.

02 · Treatment evidence

Current treatments

Two U.S.-approved complement inhibitors slow GA lesion growth and have a neovascular safety signal. Pegcetacoplan motivates our interval question.

03 · Decision setting

Why choose the next interval at each visit?

For an eye with GA, the selected interval sets the timing of the next examination and, if that examination is negative, the next dose.

Today’s visitObserve the lesion pattern and treatment history. Give the scheduled dose.
Choose the return dateAn earlier return may bring an earlier dose or earlier detection of CNV.
At the next examinationIf CNV is absent, reassess and choose again. If detected, the care pathway changes.

GA functional protection

The value of another dose may depend on how much tissue remains at risk and where that tissue is located.

CNV risk and consequences

Treatment-related risk and the time until examination both matter when weighing a shorter against a longer interval.

04 · Research gap

The gap in GA interval scheduling

We study complement-inhibitor scheduling for GA before the first CNV event in the treated eye. Existing work informs different parts of this decision.

The open decision question

As retinal status and treatment history change, when should the next visit be sooner or later, given the trade-off between GA protection and first-event CNV risk?

05 · Research direction

Dynamic interval decisions for GA

Our goal is to connect the state of tissue that can still be protected with the value of returning sooner.

Decision

Choose at each visit

Use the current retinal status and treatment history to choose the next interval, then update the decision as new observations arrive.

Trade-off

Balance benefit and harm

Consider GA-related functional protection alongside treatment-associated CNV risk, its consequences, and the burden of additional visits.

Open questions

Understand when choices change

Which states favor a shorter or longer return? How much decision loss might arise if treatment-induced CNV risk is ignored?

This page presents the clinical motivation and research questions. The model, methods, and analytical results remain under development and are not part of this public overview.

Terms in this overview

AMD · Age-related macular degeneration.

GA · Geographic atrophy, a late form of dry AMD.

nAMD · Neovascular, or wet, AMD.

CNV · Choroidal neovascularization, abnormal blood-vessel growth beneath the retina.

EOM · Every other month.

Anti-VEGF · Treatment targeting vascular endothelial growth factor to control abnormal blood-vessel activity.
